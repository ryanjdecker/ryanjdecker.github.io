Atheism

The English word atheist descends from ancient Greek ἄθεος atheos (a- 'without' + theos 'god'), which... (Whitmarsh 2021).

Atheist appears in English in the 16th century, followed in the 17th century by deist and then theist. Atheism, deism, theism, and agnosticism each appear in English after their -ist forms (Oxford English Dictionary).

Flew (1972) defines atheism as the absence of belief in gods. Negative atheists don't affirm theism. Positive atheists deny theism. Agnostics deny knowing whether God exists. While agnostics affirm the intelligibility of theism, some negative atheists don't.

Smith (1989) also defines atheism as the absence of belief in gods and distinguishes between explicit atheists who have considered the arguments for God and found them wanting and implicit atheists who have not seriously considered the arguments for theism (because they are unaware, indifferent, and so on). Explicit atheists deny or suspend judgment on theism. He defines agnosticism as the belief that it is impossible to know whether gods exist.
